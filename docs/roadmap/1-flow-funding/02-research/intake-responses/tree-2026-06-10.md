## C. Automation vs. control

- **Default: fully automatic** — once set up, flows without intervention
- **But:** contracts are negotiated in relationship, before work begins. The negotiation IS the control mechanism. Automation executes what was agreed.
- **What earns trust:** explicit agreements negotiated upfront; nothing implicit
- **What destroys trust:** ambiguity, work starting without clear terms
- **Her operating principle:** "All agreements need to be explicit and clear from the start. No work without clearly negotiated terms."
- ⟨no: she did not express fear about being drained or losing control of flows — her concern is about clarity of agreement, not control of the tap⟩

## I. Open / surprises / contradictions

**The big reorientation (verbatim):** "I'm not actually certain that this approach is what the questionnaire is wanting from me. I actually want every company that I'm a part of creating to operate with flow funding to people I don't even know."

→ This is the most important thing she said. Flow funding for Tree is not a personal finance tool. It is a **company architecture principle** applied at the level of every organization she builds.

**The contract-as-spec insight (verbatim):** "Perhaps the math on the contracts actually defines the expiration dates and the amounts should be negotiated first."

→ Elegant and load-bearing. The flow funding system doesn't need an abstract expiration policy — it reads the contract terms and executes them.

**The residual arc:** She explicitly named a career trajectory — salary-heavy now, residual-heavy later — across five income streams (salary, revenue share, book, speaking, cards). The system should be able to model and support this arc over time.

**Atlas / holomovement:** Not raised by Tree in this session. Not asked.

**Tensions to preserve:**
- Wants full automation AND wants full relational negotiation before automation runs. These aren't contradictory — negotiation sets the terms, automation executes them — but the design needs to honor both.
- Wants transparency for all flows AND operates in a world where some contributors are anonymous or unknown to her. How does transparency work when the recipient is "a practitioner who attended a circle two years ago"? Design team should think this through.

## J. Facilitator's summary for the design team

- **Flow funding for Tree is not a personal wallet feature. It is a governance and revenue architecture for every company she builds.** Design for NAO, Care Circles, and ReWoven as the primary surfaces, with Tree's personal income as an output of those systems, not the starting point.

- **The contract IS the flow spec.** She negotiates terms in relationship (amounts, revenue share %, duration), and the system executes those terms automatically. Build a contract-to-flow pipeline, not a generic threshold system.

- **Two-tier contributor model:** (1) Founders — permanent revenue share, no expiration. (2) Active contributors — flow while active, diminishes on departure. Time-limited contributors governed by contract terms.

- **Full transparency is non-negotiable.** All flows visible to all participants. Design for this from the start.

- **Her income arc is salary → residual over time.** The system should be able to model this — supporting her while active work is happening, then sustaining her through residuals as she steps back.

- **The single thing that would make or break this for her:** Clarity of agreement before work begins. "No work without clearly negotiated terms." If the system allows ambiguous or implicit flows, she won't trust it.

**Open questions for the design team to bring back to Tree:**
1. Is the ReWoven pilot consultant IOU (~$20k, 1–2% revenue share) per consultant individually, or a collective founding amount?
2. Is there a floor — a level below which she'd want support to flow in automatically? She has reserves now but didn't name a floor.
3. Is there money she wants protected from the keep-it-moving principle (emergency fund, savings for the land)?
4. Who could act for her if she's ill, traveling, or going through a hard time?
5. How does transparency work for contributors she's never met (e.g. a practitioner who attended a circle years ago)? Does "visible to all" mean visible to the whole ecosystem, or visible to the people in a given flow relationship?
6. How should the system handle fairness when there isn't enough surplus to reach everyone at once?
7. Does she want any protection for quieter/less-visible contributors who might get passed over?
