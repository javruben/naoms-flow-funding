## B. Thresholds (comfort / floor / surplus)

- **"Enough for me" (ceiling):** $15K/month for the core team of four. "My cup is full." Above this, surplus flows outward.
- **Floor (support starts coming in):** $7K/month. "If we dropped down to 7K we'd start to really struggle to focus." Below this, the work suffers because survival takes over.
- **The basics:** Rent alone is ~$3K/month for all four (including Simon). On top of that: food, occasional eating out, transport, bike repair, visa costs, similar life needs. "We all have our needs."
- **For recipients beyond core team:** $3K/month per person would be transformative.

### Thresholds are not two switches — flow is a gradient
The design brief inherited a hidden binary: receive below the floor, give above the ceiling, nothing in between. Nature doesn't work that way and neither should the protocol. **Flow forward begins at the floor and accelerates toward maximum as a holon approaches abundance**, reaching full outward flow at the ceiling. Below the floor: net receiving. Above the ceiling: full outward flow. The middle band is a smooth curve, not a dead zone.

This matters because the dead-zone model accidentally encodes "secure yourself fully before you help anyone" — the accumulation logic the protocol exists to compost. The gradient model is truer to how mutual aid actually works: people give a little while still receiving a little, and that simultaneous give-and-receive is what keeps the web alive. Almost everyone is always flowing something.

⟨open: is the *shape* of the curve (generous early vs. cautious until near abundance) set per-holon, or a network-level constant? Simon's instinct leans per-holon — consistent with trust-as-topology and "each to their own" — but this was not pinned down.⟩

### Thresholds are not static — they trend toward zero
Simon's five-year picture: rent is low because the network has acquired land, has its own food production, and strong local supplier relationships. The tech stack is open source, providing what's needed at marginal cost with no extractive capital to pay. **"My costs are trending towards zero."** As the network frees itself from rent, each person's floor drops, freeing more surplus to flow. This is the Compost Capital thesis operating as the engine of the protocol: less extraction → lower floors → more surplus → more flow capacity → more people freed. The flywheel.

In that state, Simon's own flows go "to the people building both local and global solutions that still need to cover rent" — i.e. surplus moves toward those not yet free of extraction, and toward the earth (Section D).

## C. Automation vs. control — RESOLVED: trust is the topology

This was the central question of the first sitting and was resolved in the second. **The earlier framing ("Simon directs flows from the top") was the misframing that broke the biomimicry.** Here is the resolved stance.

**Trust is what weights the relationships, and trust is what shapes the channels flow moves through.** Remove the relational element and the system doesn't become neutral — it becomes *trustless*, and money pools randomly on a flat plane. The goal is a **trust-full** system. So the relational quality isn't a feature bolted onto a watershed; it *is* the watershed's topology.

- **Simon's clarifying correction:** His earlier line — "the whole point of this protocol is that it's the first one. I know my people. If it's automated it loses its soul, and therefore its consciousness" — was given in response to a binary the facilitator posed (is it you directing, or has something else emerged?). It should **not** be read as "Simon sits at the top forever." It should be read as: *trust is irreducible, and a fully automated router would be soulless because it would be trustless.*
- **The resolved model:** Every person in the network weights their own relationships by trust. Surplus flows through those weighted channels toward need. The network-level pattern *emerges* from the sum of everyone's individual trust maps. **There is no top.** Simon decides who *he* is connected to and how wide *his* channels are. He does not decide where the network's surplus goes.
- **What each person controls:** their own topology (who they trust, channel width) and their own flows. Not the system's global allocation.
- **Why the watershed metaphor now holds:** water flows downhill through whatever channels exist; here, surplus flows toward need through whatever trust channels exist. Nobody directs the water. The topology and gravity do the work. The "return to sender vs. cascade onward" lever from the first sitting is **no longer needed** — it was an artefact of the top-down misframing, and Simon himself flagged that it had no analogue in a real watershed.

### Network boundary is perspectival
"In five years we have a network of networks, and the boundary of the network is different from every perspective. From the global perspective the membrane is global. From an individual perspective it's who I know and trust." The protocol has no single edge. Each node sees its own membrane.

### Onboarding a newcomer
A newcomer has no channels, so how do they begin to receive? **"Does someone trust them?"** One trust channel is enough. Someone already in the network opens a channel to the newcomer and stakes their own judgement on it; flow can then reach the newcomer through that person. The newcomer's topology grows as they build more relationships. This is the inspiral.

### What earns / destroys trust
- **Earns:** relational knowledge. "If I'm having conversations with these people, they can tell me what they need and I can trust them."
- **Destroys:** de-personalisation. "They could be filling in a form every month but that's not the same — mainly because it's easier to mislead a form, and when it's de-personalised, it might be more tempting to deceive. It has to be done in relationship."

## D. Fairness — RESOLVED into a phase model, plus the keystone

### The collective intelligence makes the allocation, not any individual
Simon's leverage principle — fund "the one who is most likely to be able to create value for the network once they are funded… in service to realising the network's purpose, and thus attracting more funding for others" — does **not** require a central allocator. If the architecture is right, multiple people independently recognise the same high-leverage person and flow to them. The person is funded *by the algorithm of aggregated individual trust decisions*. The collective intelligence makes the call without anyone convening to make it.

### It is not value-creation OR care — it is a phase transition
- **Scarcity / bootstrapping phase:** allocate strategically toward value creation, because that's how the network reaches abundance. "To get to that abundance we have to think strategically."
- **Abundance phase:** allocate toward unmet need — "those needs that weren't met," the people in genuine need who aren't high value-creators and would otherwise be left behind.
- **Crucially, no one has to agree which phase it is.** Each person weights their own flows ("each to their own"). The network's state — scarce or abundant — is *expressed* through the aggregate of everyone's individual weighting, not declared by a committee. If the network values care over value-creation in a given context, the most-in-need get paid first, because that's how people are choosing to weight.

### The keystone: flow to the earth
This is the deepest point Simon made, and he framed it as underpinning the entire thesis:

> "There must be flow to the earth, to the elements, and to the communities that support its flourishing, until such time as it can once again sustain itself through the life that is created, through the abundance we create together."

The market is blind to the most important work — care for the earth, the elements, the communities that make flourishing possible — because that work produces the conditions for everything else rather than a priceable output. **So the lowest point in the watershed, where flow should naturally gravitate, is precisely the regenerative work the market renders invisible.** The protocol's job is to see that low point and let flow run toward it.

The rent-freedom loop closes here: money that currently leaks to landlords (extraction without production) gets redirected to the earth, "where production is the default." Not just plugging the leak — reversing its polarity. Extraction becomes regeneration.

This also reframes demurrage (Section E): money that "wants to move on" isn't moving randomly. In a healthy network it gravitates toward regeneration, the true lowest point. Hoarding is anti-life; circulation toward the earth is the natural state.

## E. Hoarding & money-that-keeps-moving

- **Reaction to demurrage:** Curious but cautious. "It might make me want to ensure that money is invested immediately rather than sitting there becoming less valuable. So there would be less to flow on." He sees the risk that demurrage pushes toward investment (removing money from the flow network) rather than circulation. ⟨tension, live: demurrage is meant to keep money moving, but could incentivise locking it into investments instead — see Section I⟩
- **But:** "What's left should be deployed within my trusted network. I like that." And reframed by the keystone, demurrage's pull is toward regeneration, not random movement.
- **Money he wants PROTECTED:** Savings for significant collective goals — specifically **land or property purchase** to eliminate the rent floor entirely. "We need to be able to save up. Or find ways to resource these purchases as a collective." The system needs a ring-fencing mechanism for collective savings goals, exempt from demurrage.

### Pooling — nature stores for hard times
Peer-to-peer flow is not the only structure the protocol needs. Nature stores resources for lean periods — fat reserves, seed banks, soil carbon, water tables — and the protocol should model this. A common pool is a node in the topology that **stores rather than consumes, and releases under conditions**. The governing question is always *what triggers release*: a pool that only fills is hoarding with extra steps.

At least two pool types emerge, and they behave differently:
- **Buffer / common reserve pool.** Fills in good times, releases to whoever drops below their floor in hard times. Collective insurance. This is ongoing and cyclical — it breathes in and out with the network's fortunes.
- **Purpose pool / goal-pool.** Fills toward a target, then discharges once — into land, infrastructure, a purchase — after which the floor it was protecting against **drops permanently** (e.g. buying land eliminates the rent floor forever). This is the Compost Capital flywheel made concrete: a one-time discharge that lowers everyone's baseline need.

### The unifying insight: the recipient is any holon
Flow does not distinguish between *kinds* of recipient. A channel can terminate in **a person, an organisation, a piece of land, a common reserve pool, a savings account, or a purpose pool** — any holon. Topology and need do the work regardless of what sits at the end of the channel. The land you flow to is kin. The buffer pool is kin. This dissolves the apparent difference between "supporting a person" and "contributing to the commons" — they are the same gesture pointed at different holons.

### Two ways to flow into a pool (Simon wants both)
- **As a chosen destination** — a holon directs flow to the pool the same way they'd flow to any kin. Relational, voluntary, intentional.
- **As an automatic tithe** — a fraction of every flow passing through a holon siphons into the pool as it moves. This is the percentage-to-commons mechanic from the Compost Capital essay, operating continuously and in the background.

Both should be available; they serve different intentions and a healthy network likely uses both at once.

## F. Privacy & visibility — RESOLVED: bidirectional channels

- **Core principle:** Not transparency of flows, but transparency of *outcomes*. "There needs to be feedback as to what value is created from those flows in order to maintain that trust."
- **The channels are bidirectional.** Money flows downstream toward need. **Story flows upstream to maintain trust.** "There must be other signals from the network that help us to build and maintain that trust. There must be information flows that are sent upstream, to show the value that is being created, that the market cannot see."
- **This is story-as-accounting.** The story that answers what happened, who did what, what persists, and what the purpose was *is* the upstream signal. It is how unpriced work (the bioregional/regenerative work) becomes legible to the network without being priced. The quest-completion / threshold event — where living process crystallises into a record the network can hold — is the natural mechanism. **No story, no trust. No trust, no flow.** Information and money are two directions of the same channel.
- **Principal-agent problem dissolved by design:** "Ultimately we all become funders." When everyone is both funder and recipient, the surveillance dynamic disappears — you don't need to watch the money if you trust the network. "There's a sense in which the funder doesn't need to see the flows — they trust the network."
- **ZK proofs:** raised as a possible mechanism for proving need (or proving value created) without revealing underlying financial detail. ⟨unsure: how seriously to resource this vs. it being a direction worth holding⟩
- **Private relationships:** people may have legitimate reasons to keep specific flows private (raised with humour, but the requirement is real). This sits in genuine tension with the network's need for transparency — see live tension 2 below.

## G. Trust, safety, exit — RESOLVED: governance is woven, not layered

- **Stopping a flow:** "Immediately, pending appropriate governance." Simon controls his own flows and governs his own decision to stop them.
- **But governance is not a layer above the network — it is the network deliberating.** Before suspending a flow, Simon "might want to talk to my own council." That council is not a formal body; it's the people he'd naturally turn to before a hard call.
- **The deeper realisation:** the output of the protocol "will never be solely the collection of individual decisions, but the individual decisions weighted by their own relationships and conversations within the network. Inevitably, people will come together to make collective decisions on where flows go."
- **Therefore the trust topology is multi-purpose.** The same channels carry money (downstream), story (upstream), *and* deliberation (lateral). Governance, information, and value all move through the same relational fabric. This strengthens the watershed model rather than complicating it — the topology was never only about money.
- **Delegation:** Simon would delegate to a team member if unavailable (sick, traveling). No specific person named — "I'd delegate to a team member."

## I. Open / surprises / contradictions

### Surprise — Rent leakage visibility (now integral, not a side note)
"There's something here about flagging where the money is going in terms of rentier leakage, then as a network we can strategise to reduce our collective rent bill, and keep that energy in the network." This is the Compost Capital lens applied directly, and it connects to the keystone: making extractive outflow visible is the first step to redirecting that energy back into the network and ultimately to the earth.

### Tensions now RESOLVED (recorded so the design team sees the reasoning, not just the outcome)
- **Irrigation vs. watershed** → resolved. Trust is the topology; need is the gravity; nobody directs. The top-down framing was the error.
- **Value-creation vs. care** → resolved as a phase transition expressed through aggregated individual weighting, not a rule anyone imposes.
- **Individual vs. collective decision-making** → resolved. Individual decisions are already shaped by conversation within the network; collective intelligence is woven through individual choice, not layered above it.
- **Permanent vs. bridge funding** → resolved by the keystone. Permanent flow to unpriceable regenerative work is not a failure mode; it is the point. Flow gravitates there until that work can sustain itself through the abundance it helps create.
- **Funder/recipient surveillance (principal-agent)** → resolved. Everyone is both; trust replaces monitoring; outcomes (story) replace transaction-watching.

### Tensions still LIVE (for the design team to hold)
1. **Demurrage vs. investment incentive.** Demurrage keeps money circulating but may push people to lock it into investments to escape decay, reducing flow. Needs design attention — possibly via the regeneration-gravity reframing and the protected-savings carve-out, but not fully resolved.
2. **Privacy of specific edges vs. transparency that cultivates trust.** There is a legitimate individual need to keep specific flows private, and a systemic need for transparency in order to build and maintain trust. These pull against each other directly. Outcome-transparency and bidirectional story (Section F) resolve *part* of this — the network can see value created without seeing every transaction — but the residual conflict between an individual's right to a private edge and the network's need to perceive genuine need remains unresolved. ZK proofs are the candidate mechanism for proving need-or-value without revealing the underlying detail.
3. **Seeing unpriced need without a market signal.** Resolved in principle (story flows upstream through trust channels; someone who knows the work vouches for it) but the concrete mechanism — how a threshold/quest event becomes a trust-bearing signal the network acts on — is a real design build.

### Atlas / holomovement
Not raised by Simon this session. Both are already well-documented context (Atlas Research Group is one of his ventures; the holomovement / flow-as-primary intuition runs through the whole design), so no clarification needed.

## J. Facilitator's summary for the design team

### The architecture Simon actually wants (the load-bearing frame)
A **trust-full watershed**. Every participant maintains their own trust topology — who they're connected to, how wide each channel is. Surplus (above each person's ceiling) flows through those channels toward need (below each person's floor). Need is gravity; trust is terrain; nobody routes from a centre. The same channels carry money downstream, story upstream, and deliberation laterally. The whole thing trends toward zero rent over time, which lowers floors and increases flow — the Compost Capital flywheel as the protocol's engine.

### Most important, load-bearing things Simon needs:
- **Concrete anchor:** $15K/month ceiling, $7K/month floor for the core team of four. Below $7K the work stops.
- **Trust must be irreducible.** A fully automated router is unacceptable because it is trustless and therefore soulless. The system executes plumbing; relationships carry judgement.
- **Allocation emerges, it is not imposed.** Aggregated individual trust-weighted choices produce the network's allocation, including the scarcity→abundance phase shift, with no central allocator.
- **Flow ultimately gravitates to the earth** and to the unpriceable regenerative/community work the market cannot see. This is the protocol's reason for existing, not an edge case.
- **Bidirectional channels:** money down, story up. Story-as-accounting is how unpriced value becomes legible and sustains trust.
- **Rent-leakage visibility** so the network can collectively reduce extraction and redirect that energy inward and earthward.
- **Flow is a gradient, not two switches.** Outward flow begins at the floor and accelerates to maximum at the ceiling; the in-between is a curve, not a dead zone. Almost everyone is always flowing something.
- **The recipient is any holon** — person, organisation, land, buffer pool, purpose pool. Supporting a person and contributing to the commons are the same gesture aimed at different nodes.
- **Pools store for hard times.** A buffer pool (fills in good times, releases below-floor in bad) and a purpose pool (fills to a target, discharges once into land/infrastructure, permanently lowering a floor). Flow into pools both ways: chosen destination *and* automatic tithe.
- **Protected collective savings** (land purchase to eliminate rent), exempt from demurrage.

### The single thing that would make or break this:
**Whether the build preserves trust as the topology.** Simon will reject any version where an algorithm allocates without relationship — not because automation is bad, but because a trustless system has no consciousness and the protocol's entire premise is that flow follows trust. The design succeeds if relational knowledge shapes the terrain (channels, width, who's connected) while need determines direction (the gravity). It fails the moment it tries to compute allocation independent of trust.

### Open questions the design team should bring back to Simon:
1. **Concrete mechanism for upstream story → actionable trust signal.** How does a quest/threshold completion by unpriceable work become something the network perceives and flows toward?
2. **Demurrage design** that keeps money circulating without driving it into flow-escaping investments.
3. **ZK / selective privacy:** how much to invest in proving need-or-value without revealing detail.
4. **"Appropriate governance" for stopping a flow** — what does the council mechanism look like in practice, given it's woven not formal?
5. **Scope:** are the ~10 people across the two other startups inside this network or separate? Who specifically are the four bioregional members and two Foundations?
6. **Phase-shift legibility:** is any shared signal of "are we in scarcity or abundance" useful, or does it genuinely stay fully emergent and perspectival?
7. **Gradient shape:** is the flow curve from floor to ceiling set per-holon or network-wide? (Simon's instinct: per-holon.)
8. **Pool release rules:** what precisely triggers a buffer pool to release, who governs it, and how does a purpose pool's one-time discharge get authorised? Pools that only fill become hoards — release conditions are the whole design.
