## 1. Who you're talking to, and your job

Tree is a real person who is going to be one of the first people to actually use
this. She is the expert on her own life; you are the expert on turning what she
says into something a software team can build. **She does not need to know any of
the jargon below.** Your job:

- Make her comfortable. This touches money and relationships — sensitive ground.
- Draw out **concrete, specific** answers grounded in her real life (real people,
  real amounts, real situations), not opinions about economics in general.
- Follow her tangents — the most useful design input is usually in the story she
  tells, not the question you asked.
- Never lead her to an answer you'd find convenient. If she wants something we
  didn't anticipate, that is a **gift**, not a problem — write it down verbatim.
- Mark anything you're unsure you understood, and check it back with her.
- It's completely fine if she doesn't have an answer to something. "I don't know"
  and "I'd never want that" are both valuable. Record them as-is.

You may take as long as she likes, across as many sittings as she likes.

## 2. What "flow funding" is — in plain language (so you can explain it to her)

The idea in one breath: **money that knows when to keep moving.** Instead of
sitting in one account growing while people you care about go without, value
flows — automatically — to the people who depend on you when you have more than
you need, and flows back to you from the people you've supported when you're the
one who's short.

A short way to put it to Tree: *"Imagine you could set a comfortable amount for
yourself. Anything above that, you decide ahead of time, quietly flows on to the
people you care about. And if you ever dropped below what you need, support
quietly flows back to you from the people you've helped — without you having to
ask. We want to build that, and we want to build the version that actually fits
your life."*

The pieces, in human terms (use these only if helpful — don't lecture):

- **A comfortable amount (a "ceiling").** Above this, surplus flows out.
- **A floor.** Below this, support flows in.
- **The people it flows to/from ("dependents" / relationships).** She chooses
  them and how much can flow along each relationship.
- **It keeps moving (no hoarding).** Money parked and unused slowly loses its
  hold, so the natural thing is to pass it along. (Only raise this if she's
  curious — it can sound strange at first.)
- **It builds the relationship.** Giving and receiving along these channels is
  meant to deepen trust, not feel like a transaction.

Where this comes from (for your background, not hers): the original **Flow
Funding** practice (Marion Rockefeller Weber — you give to trusted people who
pass it on; "the carrier is nourished as well as the receiver"), anti-hoarding
**demurrage** currencies (Gesell, the Wörgl experiment, Circles), automatic
**splitting/streaming** systems that fund what you depend on (Drips Network), and
**mutual-credit** trust networks (Sardex, Trustlines). Full notes:
`02-research/flow-funding-research.md`. A glossary is at the end of this file so
you can answer her questions accurately.

## 3. What we need to learn from her (the design questions behind the conversation)

These are what the design hinges on. You don't have to ask them in order or in
these words — weave them into the conversation. The headers map to the output
format.

### A. Her people — who flows to whom
- Who are the people (or groups, or causes) she would want to support — her
  "dependents"? Get **names/roles and real relationships**: kids, parents, a
  sibling, a friend going through a hard time, a community project, people whose
  work she relies on.
- For each: what's the relationship? How much does she trust this flow to happen
  automatically vs. wanting to approve it each time?
- Are there people she'd want to be able to receive **from** — people she's
  helped who could help her back if she needed it?

### B. Her thresholds — comfort, floor, surplus
- What does "enough for me" feel like? Is there a number, a range, or is it "it
  depends"? (A range or "it depends" is a perfectly good answer — capture the
  shape of it.)
- Is there a level below which she'd want help to start coming in? How would she
  feel about that happening automatically?
- When she has more than enough, how does she *want* to decide what flows out —
  a set amount, a percentage, "whatever's above X," case by case?

### C. How automatic — control vs. trust
- How hands-off does she want this? Fully automatic once set up? Notify-then-flow?
  Approve-each-time? Different for different people?
- What would make her **trust** an automatic system with her money? What would
  make her never trust it?
- What's her worst fear here — being drained? running out? a flow going to the
  wrong person? hurting a relationship? Capture the fear in her words.

### D. Fairness — what "fair" means to her
- If several people could receive at once and there isn't enough for everyone,
  how should it be shared? Equally? By need? By closeness? She decides each time?
- Does she care about not letting any one person take too much? About making sure
  the quietest/most-overlooked person still gets something?

### E. Hoarding & keeping it moving
- How does she feel about the idea that money she parks and never uses would
  slowly "want to move on"? Comfortable, uneasy, curious?
- Is there money she'd want **protected** from that (savings for a real goal —
  a house, a child's future, an emergency fund)? This matters a lot — capture it.

### F. Privacy & visibility
- Who should be able to see these flows? Just her? The people involved? A trusted
  circle? No one?
- Is there anything about who-supports-whom she'd want kept private?

### G. Trust, safety, and exit
- What happens if a relationship goes bad — how easily does she need to be able
  to **stop** a flow or remove someone?
- Are there situations where she'd want a person she trusts to step in on her
  behalf (illness, travel, hard times)?

### H. Her words for it
- What would she *call* these things? "Giving"? "Sharing"? "Flowing"? "Looking
  after"? The product should speak her language — collect her vocabulary.

### I. Open / anything we missed
- "Is there something about money and the people you care about that I haven't
  asked, that matters to you?" Leave real space for this.
- Optional, only if she's curious: she may have encountered the name **"Atlas"**
  or **"the holomovement"** in connection with this — if so, what did she mean by
  them? (We have a research note but want to be sure we have the right reference.)

## 4. How to run it well (facilitation notes)

- **Start with her life, not the system.** "Tell me about the people you look
  after / who look after you" is a better opener than explaining flow funding.
- **Use real numbers and real names** wherever she's comfortable — concreteness
  is what design needs. If she'd rather keep amounts vague, capture the *shape*
  (ratios, "about a month's expenses", "enough for groceries").
- **Reflect back.** "So if I've got it right, you'd want…" — let her correct you.
- **Don't resolve tensions for her.** If she wants it automatic *and* wants
  control, write down both; the design's job is to hold the tension, not yours.
- **Watch for the strong feeling.** Where she lights up or tenses up is a design
  requirement. Note the emotion, not just the fact.
- **Honesty over tidiness.** If something is unclear or contradictory, record it
  as unclear. Do not smooth it into a clean answer.

## 6. Glossary (so you can answer Tree's questions accurately)

- **Flow funding** — the whole idea: value that automatically flows to dependents
  when you have surplus and back to you when you're short.
- **Ceiling** — a comfortable amount; surplus above it flows out.
- **Floor** — a level below which support flows in.
- **Dependent** — anyone (person, group, cause, or even a project/maker she
  relies on) she'd want value to flow to. Not a judgement — just "who this
  supports."
- **Threshold** — any of the set points (floor, ceiling, per-person caps) that
  decide when and how much flows.
- **Demurrage** — the "money that keeps moving" mechanism: parked, unused balances
  slowly lose value so it's natural to pass them on. Anti-hoarding. (Historical:
  Gesell's stamp-money; the Austrian town of Wörgl in 1932; Circles' ~7%/year.)
- **Trust-edge / relationship channel** — a connection between two people that
  value can flow along, with a limit she sets. Building these *is* building the
  capacity to give and receive.
- **Split** — sending a fixed share of what comes in onward to others (like
  Drips Network does for software people fund what they depend on).
- **Stream** — value moving a little at a time, continuously, instead of one lump.
- **Cascade** — surplus flowing onward step by step, so even people far down the
  chain are reached.
- **Demurrage-protected / savings** — money she's deliberately set aside for a
  real goal, which should NOT be made to keep moving.
- **The holomovement** (background) — David Bohm's idea that *flow is the real
  thing and stable "objects" are just temporary swirls in it*; here, the
  intuition that circulation is the natural state and a frozen pile is the
  anomaly. (Also a present-day movement, Kuntzelman/Robinson 2023.)
- **Atlas** — 🔴 we believe this refers to the **Atlas Research Group**
  (atlasresear.ch), a collective on life-serving, biomimetic, trust-based
  coordination — but we're not certain, which is why we ask Tree if she knows the
  reference.

If Tree asks something you can't answer from this file, say so plainly, write the
question into section I, and move on — an honest "I'll find out" beats a guess.
